        Sky from the site

        Why it changes

        A day on the Moon lasts about 29.5 Earth days, so the Sun crawls across the sky. At the poles it circles the horizon only a degree or two up, so one hill can block it for hours. Earth stays in almost the same spot, wobbling a few degrees, so at the edge of the near side it rises and sets about every two weeks.

        Past missions

        Every Apollo landing was near the middle of the side facing Earth.

        Flat ground, Earth high in the sky, and a landing in the lunar morning. The next landings aim for the south pole, which is a very different place.

        Apollo sites (near the equator)

        • Landed in the lunar morning, with the Sun low in the east
        • Earth high in the sky the whole time: easy radio contact
        • Flat lava plains, chosen for safe landing
        • About 14 Earth days of daylight, then about 14 of night

        South pole (where we are going)

        • The Sun circles the horizon, only a degree or two up
        • Earth sits low and can set for days at a time
        • Ridges and crater rims; some stay sunlit much of the year
        • Deep crater floors never see the Sun and may hold water ice
