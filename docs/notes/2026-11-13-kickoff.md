# Kickoff, 13 November 2026

Project points from the team's first meeting. Decisions from it are in `docs/decisions.md`.

## The idea

- A local browser web app for picking Moon landing sites.
- Core flow: enter lunar coordinates and a time; see where the Sun and Earth are, on a rotating 3D Moon.
- Possible extras: shaded ideal landing zones, double-click for precise spots, gestures to rotate the Moon.

## Data

- JPL Horizons already computes Sun and Earth positions.
- Moon Trek for terrain height.
- A third resource, from the Canadian Space Agency, was not yet understood. (It is the CSA's Moon
  Exploration portal: background reading, not data. See `docs/challenge.md`.)
- A mentor suggested estimating terrain from radio waves; the team judged existing data enough.

## What makes it ours

- Rank sites by uninterrupted sunlight: 6 hours continuous beats 7 hours with a 1-hour break.
- Earth visibility, so rovers can send data back. Seasonal Sun distance affects power.
- A "Street View" of the sky: look around a site and step forward in time.
- Side-by-side comparison of sites: pros, cons, user constraints, temperature, past Apollo landing sites.
- User inputs: camera or eye height, antenna height, solar-panel height.
- Help find a place and time that meet a set of needs.

## Judges

- Expect "why not use Horizons directly?" Answer: accessibility. Pictures for people who cannot read raw
  tables.
